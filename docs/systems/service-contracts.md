# Service contracts (stored contract wording for NEW services)
_Last verified against code: 2026-10-26 — Claude (dev job `d2971d11`, flexible-offer slice 2 step B, SANDBOX — Antonio runs the production migration and ships). **Status: fully built and proven on the sandbox — the store, the lock, the contract-page rendering, the send / Activate-Lead gates and the frozen copy (parts 1-2) AND the screens (part 3): the Contract card on the service page, the review / edit / history pop-up, "Draft with Claude" and "Ask Claude to change something", owner-only approval, and the "This offer can't be sent yet" pop-up. Decisions of 2026-10-26: the OWNERS approve (`isOwnerOnly`), a "Draft with Claude" button, changes by asking Claude AND by editing the text directly (`**bold**` marks).** **Bug-hunt pass (same day, 2 hunters): the wording is now frozen ONLY at the real send; plain offers never touch the new column; original services never get a Contract card; see the rules below.**_

## What it is
Every service in the Service Catalog needs a contract the client signs. The eight built-in short contracts (`tax_return`, `itin`, `closure`, `company_change_name`, `ein_change_name`, `shipping`, `public_notary`, `consulting_call`) have their wording in code (`SERVICE_CONTENT` in `app/offer/[token]/contract/standalone-service-agreement.tsx`). A **NEW service** (created in the Service Catalog by staff, no developer) gets its own contract type `custom_<slug>` and its wording is **stored in the database, drafted (by Claude or a person), approved by an owner, and frozen on every offer it is sold on**. Before this, a new service had no contract type: its line silently vanished from what the client signed.

## Business rules
- **Only an owner approves** (`isOwnerOnly` in `lib/auth.ts`: Antonio, plus anyone in `NEXT_PUBLIC_EXTRA_OWNER_EMAILS`). `POST /api/service-contracts/versions/[id]/approve` (`requireOwner`) enforces it; `approveDraft` only RECORDS the email it is given. A non-owner admin sees the pop-up with Approve greyed out and "Only the owner can approve a contract.".
- **Approved wording can never be edited.** A change is a new version; the old one becomes "replaced". Enforced by the database (trigger), not by application code.
- **At most one open draft and one approved version per service** (partial unique indexes).
- **Approval is bound to what was read:** the approve call carries the fingerprint (md5 of the wording, set by the database) the approver saw; if the draft changed since, the database answers `DRAFT_CHANGED` and nothing is approved.
- **An offer is never sent without approved wording:** `offerServicesReadinessProblem` (used by `publishOffer` and Activate Lead) refuses a new service with no contract type ("the client would sign nothing"), a draft only ("needs the owner's approval"), no versions, or an unknown type. A service on a BUILT-IN contract type needs no stored wording.
- **Each offer keeps a frozen copy** of the wording it was sent with (`offers.contract_texts`). The contract page renders from that copy, never from the live store, so a newer version never reaches an offer already sent.
- **WHEN the copy is taken (`ensureOfferContractTexts(token, {refresh, persist})`):** (1) at the REAL send — `publishOffer` and Activate Lead call it with `refresh: true`, which takes a FRESH copy of today's approved wording and replaces anything an earlier preview or failed attempt left; (2) a staff PREVIEW of a draft offer (`/api/offers/view`, status `draft`) calls it with `persist: false`: it shows today's approved wording and stores NOTHING; (3) a CLIENT's view (the portal shows drafts as "Proposal ready", and a draft can be signed) of a `draft`/`sent`/`viewed` offer that has no copy completes it (the safety net; never replaces an entry already there), and so does a staff view of a `sent`/`viewed` offer — staff then see what the client has; (4) a `signed`/paid/active offer is NEVER touched by the view route. Activate Lead refreshes only a DRAFT offer (an offer already out keeps the wording it carries). An ordinary offer (no new service) reads only the long-standing columns — it never touches `contract_texts` nor the contract store, so the code is safe to run before the database change exists. 'Sent with N offers' counts only offers whose status is not `draft`.
- **The client never sees an internal problem.** If an offer's wording is missing when the client opens it, the page shows "Your agreement is being prepared" (no contract, no signature box) and staff are told in the team chat (channel `td-bug`, once per offer per 6 hours) with the fix.
- **Claude only PROPOSES.** "Draft with Claude" / "Ask Claude to change something" (`lib/contracts/draft-with-claude.ts`, model `claude-opus-5-5`) return wording that is checked by the same validator as storage, saved as a DRAFT, and becomes real only when an owner approves it. Staff-entered text (service name, description, steps, the change request) goes to Claude fenced as data; an invalid answer is retried once with the exact problems, then refused ("Nothing was saved").
- **Editing is on a draft only, in place.** Every line of the contract is a text box (`ContractEditor`); problems are shown under their own line (`validateContractWording` returns `fields` keyed like `scope.items.1`); changes autosave when valid; "Done editing" shows the contract as the client reads it. An approved version is never edited — "Start a new version" copies it into a new draft. Saves carry the fingerprint they started from (`expectedHash`), so two tabs cannot silently overwrite each other.
- **`**bold**`:** words between two stars are bold (React `<strong>`, never HTML). Allowed only on lines that can carry it (scope intro/items, procedure intro/step text, commitments, client-responsibility intro and the text after the bold heading); refused elsewhere (titles, closing sentence, acceptance sentence, after-signing message, the two phrases). A client responsibility is ONE line in the editor, `**heading** rest` ↔ `{bold, rest}`; the space after the heading is kept and never lost.
- **The work / liability phrases are required** (the page falls back to the TAX-RETURN sentence without them).
- **An offer is refused — with the exact fix — in two more cases (readiness, `serviceReadinessBlockers`):** the offer is set up with a contract that cannot carry the service's own agreement (typically built while the service had none, so it fell back to the formation contract: "delete this draft offer and create it again"; a REAL formation/onboarding offer — one that has a line of that kind (a line typed `formation`, or the untyped `Company Formation` / `Client Onboarding` pipeline line the assistant tools create), judged per package option — carries the new service's agreement as an add-on and is fine; an offer with no type is judged as the formation contract, like the page does; this blocker has no link because the fix is to rebuild the offer), and the existing ones (no steps / switched off / no approved contract). The CRM Send button, Activate Lead and the lead page show them in the "This offer can't be sent yet" pop-up with an "Open contract" link; the MCP tools get the same sentence.
- **An offer built BEFORE the contract existed** has service lines with no contract type. At send time (and the first time the client's page asks) `ensureOfferContractTexts` gives those lines the service's own type from the live catalog (never overwriting a type), then freezes the wording.
- **Only NEW services get a Contract card** (`contractEligibility` in `lib/contracts/service-contract-view.ts`): a service with its own pipeline that is not one of the original services. The original services (EIN, DBA, Banking Fintech, CMRA, Annual Renewal, Certificate of Incumbency …) are `legacy`, a service with no pipeline is `no_pipeline`, the eight built-in contracts and the full formation/onboarding agreements are `built_in`, an unknown typed value is `other` — each shows the server's plain sentence instead of a button, and `POST …/draft` refuses them (giving an original service a contract type would change the contract of EVERY offer containing it). Checked against the real production catalog on 2026-10-26: no existing service is a 'new' one, so nothing existing is affected.
- **Two services never share a contract type:** `ensureContractType` refuses a type another service already has (`TYPE_IN_USE`) and one typed by hand that is not the one its own slug gives; the service page refuses a hand-typed `custom_*` (`typedCustomContractTypeProblem`).
- **Saving the service page never unlinks its own contract:** `contractTypeToKeep` keeps `custom_*` whatever a stale page sends, and the Contract type box is locked once it is set.
- **Plain text only:** wording may not contain `<` or `>`; lengths and list sizes are capped (`lib/contracts/contract-wording.ts`). The one place that builds HTML by hand (the "after signing" panel) escapes the success message too.

## How it's built
- **Tables / columns (migration `scripts/migrations/20261026-1000-service-contract-texts.sql`, + `.UNDO.sql`, applied to SANDBOX; production is run by Antonio):** `service_contract_texts` (id, `service_entry_id` → `catalog_entries`, `contract_type` `^custom_[a-z0-9_]+$`, `version` (set by the trigger), `status` draft/approved/superseded, `content` jsonb, `content_hash`, `source` claude_draft/claude_change/manual/session, `note`, `created_by`, `approved_by/at`, `superseded_at`); trigger `service_contract_texts_guard` (insert only as draft; approved/superseded immutable; delete only drafts); function `approve_service_contract_text(id, expected_hash, approved_by)` (atomic; errors `DRAFT_CHANGED`, `NOT_A_DRAFT`, `NO_APPROVER`, `NOT_FOUND`); `offers.contract_texts jsonb`. RLS on, no grants to anon/authenticated, `marketing_lock_all_tables()` called (repo rule).
- **Key files:** `lib/contracts/contract-wording.ts` (the wording shape + `validateContractWording` + `wordingFromSnapshot`; pure, client-safe), `lib/contracts/service-contract-texts.ts` (server: `ensureContractType`, `createDraft`, `saveDraft`, `discardDraft`, `approveDraft`, `getApproved`, `getApprovedByType`, `listVersions`, `contractStatusForEntry`), `lib/offers/standalone-contract-types.ts` (`isCustomContractType`, `customContractTypeForSlug`, `isStandaloneAgreementType` also true for `custom_*`, `isBuiltInAgreementType`), `lib/offers/contract-texts-snapshot.ts` (`customTypesOnOffer`, `mergeSnapshot`, `ensureOfferContractTexts`), `lib/offers/contract-wording-alert.ts`, `lib/offers/service-readiness.ts` (`contractProblem`), `contractWordingFor(type, offer)` in the standalone contract component (built-in from code, custom from the offer's copy).
- **Data flow:** (1) staff create the service in the Service Catalog (steps etc.); (2) a first draft (`createDraft`) gives the service `metadata.contract_type = custom_<slug>` (via `updateMetadata`, with a reason) — offer lines pick it up from the catalog; (3) an owner approves; (4) `publishOffer` → readiness gate → `ensureOfferContractTexts` freezes the approved version onto the offer; Activate Lead does the same; `/api/offers/view` is the safety net (first client view of an offer with no copy); (5) the contract page (`StandaloneServiceAgreement`) renders the copy through the same `AgreementBody` as the built-in contracts, so signing, the PDF, the invoice and activation are unchanged (they only need `isStandaloneAgreementType`); (6) the invoice at signing is named after the service (`getInvoiceDescription`, no more "Service Package").
- **Screens (part 3):** `components/service-contracts/` — `contract-card.tsx` (the card on `/service-catalog/[slug]/edit`, anchor `#contract`: none / draft / approved, built-in and unknown-type notes), `contract-dialog.tsx` (review, approve, edit, ask Claude, history), `contract-editor.tsx` (one text box per line), `contract-preview.tsx` (the contract as the client reads it, styles scoped under `.contract-preview`; a test fails if a class the agreement body uses is missing), `contract-api.ts`. The agreement body itself lives in `components/contracts/agreement-body.tsx` (`AgreementBody`, `InlineText`) and is shared with the client page, so the preview cannot drift from it. `components/offers/offer-blocked-dialog.tsx` is the refusal pop-up (used by `account-offer-panel`, `lead-actions`, `activate-lead-dialog`).
- **Routes (all admin; approve = owner):** `GET /api/service-contracts/[entryId]` (status, every version with offers-sent counts, viewer.canApprove, aiConfigured) · `POST …/[entryId]/draft` (`{mode:"claude"|"from_approved"}`) · `PUT|DELETE …/versions/[id]` (save an edit / throw away a draft) · `POST …/versions/[id]/ask` · `POST …/versions/[id]/approve`. Errors answer `{error, code, details?, fields?}` in plain words; a database without the migration answers 503 "not switched on yet" and the card says so instead of crashing. Helpers: `lib/contracts/service-contract-view.ts` (`loadContractView`, `loadServiceFacts`, `kindOfContractType`), `lib/contracts/service-contract-texts.ts` (`offersSentByVersion` counts offers by version AND fingerprint).
- **Where else the type is known:** the MCP `offer_create` tool accepts `custom_<service>`; the Confirm Payment pop-up lists only the built-in types when there is NO offer (with an offer it uses the offer's own type) — a new service is not in that no-offer list (not covered).

## Gotchas, invariants & past bugs
- **NO value-list CHECK constraints on this table — on purpose (2026-10-26 incident).** The first version of the migration had `CHECK (status IN (...))` / `CHECK (source IN (...))`. On the SHARED sandbox those made the CI step "Sandbox still enforces what production enforces" (`npm run check:db-contract:live`) report two `constraint_unregistered` failures on EVERY other session's pull request until the code registered them and the committed production snapshot was refreshed. The two value lists now live in the lock trigger (`service_contract_texts_guard`), which gives the same protection with nothing to register; `tests/unit/service-contract-texts-migration.test.ts` keeps them identical to `CONTRACT_TEXT_STATUSES` / `CONTRACT_TEXT_SOURCES` and fails if a value-list CHECK comes back. Verified: with the table on the sandbox the live check says "code and database agree". Same rule for any future sandbox table: run `npx tsx scripts/check-db-constraints.ts --source=supabase` right after applying a migration.
- A client responsibility's text after the bold heading starts with a space; validators that trim would glue the words ("documentswithin") — `rest` keeps its leading space and a missing one is added.
- This project's TypeScript settings do not narrow a `{ok: true} | {ok: false}` union on the boolean — use `'errors' in res`.
- `delete from service_catalog` (the view) only DEPRECATES the entry — test data needs a direct delete of `catalog_entries` (and its `catalog_decision_log` rows). Test cleanup of this table needs the guard trigger disabled by the owner role.
- The migration is safe to ship before or after the code: an ordinary offer never reads the new column or the table; once a new service exists, send/readiness reads them and refuses, closed and in plain words ('not switched on yet'), if they are missing. (An earlier version read the column for EVERY offer — found by the bug hunter, fixed.)
- On the formation / onboarding pages (`page.tsx`, `service-agreement.tsx`) a new service's add-on agreement renders through the shared `AgreementBody` (approved phrases, no-charge wording) and, if its wording is missing, the WHOLE page shows "being prepared" and takes no signature (`ContractNotReady`). Known and accepted: with the "Several agreements in one contract" switch OFF, an offer with TWO new services (or one plus a built-in) shows ONE agreement — the creation-time warning is the only guard (Antonio's call pending); two overlapping writers of the frozen copy can lose one entry (the client's page refills it); a tightened validator would make an old frozen entry show 'being prepared'.
- A back-to-back double load of the contract page (React dev mode) can post the staff alert twice; production loads once.
